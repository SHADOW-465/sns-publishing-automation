| 3. Unified detection | **Done, with limits** | Every style role is now decided by `structure/score.ts`: each signal (saved, nav, printed contents, tag, name, pattern, typography, position, rare) votes for a role and the votes combine. The old first-match result from `inferProfile` is kept as one `fallback` vote of 0.25. The InDesign and PDF readers both fill a `BlockFeatures` record (`structure/features.ts`, `indesign/features.ts`, `pdfepub/features.ts`) and the scorer reads only that record. The source's own nav and the printed contents count as one vote (one list shown in two places). Thresholds: 0.85 and up silent, 0.6 to 0.85 listed, under 0.6 blocks until decided. Notes (`structure/notes.ts`): InDesign footnote, InDesign endnote, typed endnote section (numbers matched per chapter) and PDF footnote (superscript plus numbered foot line on the same page); a reference without a note or a note without a reference is flagged at 0.4 to 0.5. Pictures (`indesign/placement.ts`): anchor, layout page marker, caption match against the print PDF; each picture records the signal that placed it and a confidence, and a picture nothing places lists the three closest printed pages. Added cross-checks: the contents lists N chapters but M were found, a chapter starts on a different page than the contents prints, chapter pages do not rise. **Limits:** the fourth picture signal (print-PDF image position by hash) is not built, because the build never decodes the print PDF's pictures. Typography gives every heading-like style h3, not a level by size rank (a rank would change the Cambia output against the hand-finished EPUB). No per-block exceptions: a block whose own signal disagrees with its style is not split off (the PDF reader still has its per-line `forced` headings). The dictionary has no entries for BU, Zitat, Kasten. PDF footnotes that have a raised number and a numbered foot line on the same page are now rendered as real notes under their section (`extractFootnotes`); an unmatched number stays as it is and is flagged. Weights are hand-set; the three reference PDFs and two InDesign books reproduce every old role. |
